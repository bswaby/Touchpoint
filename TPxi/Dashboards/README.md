# 📊 Dashboards

**Your numbers, on one page, without asking anyone to run a report.**

Ready-made dashboards for attendance, giving, membership, engagement, data health and more.
Seventeen arrive built, or drag your own together in a couple of minutes. Everything on them is
a real report you can point at your own people, your own ministry area, your own date range.

## The Monday morning problem

Somebody wants to know how attendance is doing. So somebody else builds a query, exports it,
pastes it into a spreadsheet, and emails it round. Next month, same question, same hour.

Meanwhile the answers that would change a decision sit in the database because nobody has time
to go and look: who has stopped coming, who is serving with minors without a current background
check, which emails are bouncing.

**A dashboard is only useful if the number on it is one you trust.** Most of the work here went
into that, not into the charts.

---

## What it does

Four things, and you can use any one of them without the others.

### 01 · Puts the answers on a page

Seventeen dashboards install ready to use, including Church Overview, Attendance Health,
Giving, Data Health, Volunteers and Serving and Tech Status. Each one opens on numbers a
particular person actually needs, with the names behind them a click away.

### 02 · Lets you narrow it without editing anything

Three controls sit at the top: a **date range**, a **ministry area**, and a **Search Builder
search**. Change one and every tile that can honor it follows. A children's minister points the
same dashboard at their own area and their own people.

### 03 · Turns a list into work

Any tile that lists people lets you tick names and **tag them or create tasks** in bulk, without
leaving the page. A report that tells you 47 people have lapsed is information; a report that
lets you assign those 47 to a staff member is work getting done.

### 04 · Lets you build your own, by dragging

The seventeen are a starting point, not a menu. Start a blank dashboard and **drag tiles where
you want them, drag an edge to resize**. What you see is the layout, and it is yours: no grid
settings, no column counts, nothing saved somewhere else.

## Build your own

### Drag it into shape

Click **Edit** and the page becomes a canvas. **Grab a tile by its header to move it. Grab an
edge to resize it.** Put the number that matters at the top, make the chart wide, tuck the long
list underneath. Group tiles onto **tabs** you name, so one dashboard can hold this month's
numbers, the people to follow up, and the detail nobody needs every day.

Three things worth knowing about it:

- **Tiles can size themselves.** Flip a tile to *auto* and it grows to fit its content, so a
  short list is not stranded in a tall box. Flip it to *fixed* and the content scrolls instead.
  Your choice is remembered, and auto-growing never overwrites the height you set.
- **It follows the screen.** On a narrow window, tiles stack so they stay readable.
- **It will not let you wreck your own layout.** Because a narrow screen stacks tiles, editing is
  switched off there. Saving a stacked arrangement over the one you built on a wide monitor is
  the sort of quiet damage nobody can undo, so it simply is not allowed.

### Then fill it, from five sources

| Source | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Reports** | Any of the 158 catalog reports, filtered how you like and shown how you like |
| **Build one** | Your own chart from a Search Builder search, no SQL and no report writing |
| **Checks** | A best-practice data check, as a count or a worklist |
| **TouchPoint widgets** | Any home-page widget you already have |
| **Links** | A panel of shortcuts, with icons and headings |

### Build one, without writing a report

Pick who, what to measure, and how to group it. That is the whole thing.

> *Who:* a Search Builder search, or everyone.
> *What:* attendance, giving, involvements, tasks and notes, or demographics.
> *Measured how:* totals, distinct people, averages, still-open, and so on.
> *Grouped by:* month, program, division, involvement, member type, fund, campus, age band,
> gender, member status, and more.
> *Shown as:* bar, line, pie, donut, table, or a single number.

Narrow it further by campus or member status, **Preview** it as it will actually appear, then add
it. Click any bar and it lists the people behind it, which you can then tag or raise tasks on.

Built tiles follow the dashboard's date range and search like everything else.

**One honest limit.** A built tile lives in the dashboard you made it on. If you build something
good enough to want twice, it is worth asking for it as a catalog report instead, where it gets
help text, a version, and reaches every church.

### Shortcut panels

A **Links** tile holds the handful of places this dashboard is really about: a report, an
involvement, a saved search, anything with an address. Each link takes an icon from a picker of
163, links can sit under collapsible headings, and any link can be limited to roles so staff only
see what applies to them.

## Built to be boring

> A dashboard that quietly shows the wrong number is worse than no dashboard, because people
> act on it. These are the rules the numbers follow.

✓ **Head counts are counted.** Where a meeting has a head count larger than the individual
check-ins, the head count wins. Without it most of Sunday morning is invisible. One church's
worship program logged 4,780 check-ins against 177,325 people actually counted.

✓ **A Sunday is a Sunday, not a service.** An involvement meeting three times on a Sunday shows
one Sunday, not three. Both figures are there when you want them.

✓ **Age is measured when it happened.** A safeguarding report asks who was an adult *at the
meeting*, not who is an adult today. Otherwise a nineteen-year-old is flagged for a class they
attended at sixteen.

✓ **A tile that can't follow a filter says so.** An amber tag on the tile, and the control hides
itself entirely on a tab where nothing can use it. A number that refuses to move is never a
mystery.

✓ **It matches the report your staff already read.** Weekly attendance counts the same
involvements as TouchPoint's own Week At A Glance, so the dashboard and that report agree. You
can switch it to count everything instead. Both are legitimate, and the setting says which.

✓ **Church-specific values are settings, not assumptions.** What counts as serving, how long a
background check lasts, when your fiscal year starts, which approvals count as cleared. A report
that depends on one says so, and warns when nobody has confirmed it.

## Is it for you

**A good fit if**

- Staff ask the same questions every month and someone re-runs the same query
- You want a home page that opens on numbers instead of a menu
- People need lists they can act on, not just totals
- Different ministries want the same view of their own area
- You want to drag your own page into shape rather than take what you are given

**Less useful if**

- One person runs all reporting and is happy in Search Builder
- You need pixel-exact printed reports for a board pack
- Your questions are one-offs rather than recurring

---

## Who sees what

Dashboards are personal by default. Share one and you choose which roles can open it.

**Access is checked when a tile runs, not when it is placed.** Someone can share a dashboard
with a giving tile on it, and a person outside the finance team simply sees *"Not shown. This
data is limited to your finance team."* Sharing a layout never shares data.

## Practical

| | |
|---|---|
| **Setup** | One script pasted into TouchPoint. Open it, pick a dashboard from the Library, done. |
| **Runs** | On demand. Tiles cache for a period you set, so a dashboard left open doesn't re-query all day. |
| **Needs** | A TouchPoint site. Reports and dashboards come from a shared catalog; nothing else to install. |
| **Updates** | The script tells you when a newer version is published, and can install it for you. |
| **Cost** | Free. |

### Installing

1. **Admin → Advanced → Special Content → Python**
2. **Add New**, name it `TPxi_Dashboards`, paste the script, Save
3. Go to `/PyScriptForm/TPxi_Dashboards`
4. Open **Library** and add a dashboard. *Church Overview* is a good first one
5. Open **Settings** and confirm the values your reports depend on: fiscal year start, background
   check validity, which background-check approvals count as cleared

Step 5 is worth the two minutes. Those settings change what the numbers mean, and a report that
uses one will tell you when it hasn't been confirmed.

<details>
<summary><strong>The three filters, and why some tiles ignore them</strong></summary>

Each control narrows every tile that can honor it and leaves the rest alone.

| Filter | Narrows | Tiles it can't touch |
|---|---|---|
| **Date range** | 67 of 158 reports | Current-state lists, such as a pending queue or an account roster |
| **Area** (Program) | 25 of 158 | Anything that does not record which program a number came from |
| **Search** (Search Builder) | 98 of 158 | Anything counting meetings rather than people |

That last one is worth explaining, because it looks like a bug and isn't. A head-counted
service knows 1,243 people attended; it does not know *who they were*. There is no person to
match a search against. Those tiles narrow by **area** instead, so between the two almost
everything narrows: charts by area, name lists by search.

A tile that can't follow a control is tagged in amber. A control that nothing on the tab can use
hides itself. Where it applies to only some tiles, it says so: *"All areas (5 of 7 tiles)"*.

A filter set on a tile itself always wins over the bar, and shows as a blue tag on the tile.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Weekly attendance and Week At A Glance</strong></summary>

TouchPoint's own [Week At A Glance](https://docs.touchpointsoftware.com/SummaryReports/WeekAtAGlanceSpecs.html)
report includes a Program when it has a **Report Group**, and a Division under it when that
Division has a **Report Line**.

By default this counts the same involvements, so the dashboard and that report agree. Switching
**Settings → Weekly attendance counts** to *every meeting* adds everything deliberately left off
it, such as choir and orchestra rehearsals and enrichment classes.

Both are legitimate; they answer different questions. At one church the gap is about 750 a week.
The point is that the setting says which question you're asking, rather than the two reports
quietly disagreeing.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Settings that change what numbers mean</strong></summary>

| Setting | Affects |
|---|---|
| **Fiscal year start month** | Every fiscal-year date range |
| **Weekly attendance counts** | Week At A Glance involvements, or every meeting |
| **Background check validity** | When a check counts as expired |
| **Background check approvals** | Which approval values mean cleared. Until set, reports assume *Approved* only and say so on the tile |
| **What counts as serving** | Which member types count as serving, per church |

Reports declare which of these they use, and it's shown when you add one as a tile.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>For administrators</strong></summary>

**Everyone's dashboards.** Admins can list every dashboard, personal and shared, and open one to
fix or remove it. The bar names whose dashboard it is for the whole session, so it is never
covert. Data access is still checked per tile, so opening someone's board never grants their
permissions.

**Caching.** Set per dashboard, default 30 minutes. **Settings → Is the cache working?** reports
per tile whether it came from cache and, if not, why.

**Library updates.** Installed dashboards and reports track their published definitions. A count
on the Library button means something upstream has changed; updating a report leaves your layouts
alone, and updating a dashboard you've edited warns you first.

**Script updates.** The script checks the published version and offers to install a newer one.
The comparison is numeric per component, so a copy that is ahead of what's published stays quiet.

</details>
